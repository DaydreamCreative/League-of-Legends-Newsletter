Preseason Power Rankings: The Empire, The Ghosts, and the Long Walk of John Vaughan

Another year, another blank slate that I am contractually obligated as this league's historian to fill with cold, hard, mostly-favorable-to-me truth. Let the record show that I, Matt Waters, sit atop the all-time win rate at point six nine seven with three championships to my name, which means every single word below is technically journalism and only slightly a victory lap. I don't make the numbers. I just happen to be really good at making the numbers.

The story of this league has always been the same story: can anyone dethrone the empire, and will John Vaughan's 2020 ring ever stop being the most confusing sentence in the record book. Elijah Cohen is still trying to climb out of the crater he dug in 2020 and 2021 after that glorious 2016 coronation. Ryan Bartlett keeps building the best team nobody lets win it all. And down in the Sleeper era standings, names like JS N My Pants and KEEP POUNDING are quietly stacking win rates with zero hardware to show for it, which is either admirable or a curse, depending on who you ask. I asked. Nobody had a good answer.

Meanwhile the Battle of the Johns rages on eternal, Drew Knight remains the most dangerous bad record in football, and somewhere out there Zach Smith's ghost is laughing at all of us. Let's get to the rankings, which I promise are fair, mostly, in spirit.

The Rankings

  1. Straight up Yorkin' it (Matt W). Look, I tried to be humble about this and the numbers simply would not let me. All time win rate of point six nine seven, the best mark among active managers, three championships including the 2019 masterpiece with Lamar and CMC and that league record two hundred five point one eight week, plus the 2021 title and the 2023 Sleeper era crown with Njigba's in Paris. My draft board leans Bijan and Kupp and St. Brown, a steady RB WR foundation, and I stay moderately active on waivers without ever mortgaging the roster in trades. Dynasty behavior. I did not write these rankings, the record book did. I only held the pen.
  2. KEEP POUNDING (Elijah Cohen). The founding king of 2016 has quietly rebuilt himself into a Sleeper era killer, twenty six and sixteen since 2022 after those dark seasons at three and ten and three and eleven. No Sleeper title yet, but he is drafting Gibbs and Josh Allen at the top and stays plenty involved in trades, nine of them in one stretch as Trumpet Trev alone. This is a redemption arc with real receipts behind it.
  3. Max's moms name (Matt R). Point five seven one all time and a Sleeper era title in 2024 with Olamide Zaccheaus, built on Jonathan Taylor and Garrett Wilson round two value. He has bounced between QB and TE early and WR heavy more recently with Malik Nabers and Brian Thomas, and he is not shy about the trade market, seven deals under his most recent alias. A genuine threat every single year, just usually standing slightly behind me when the confetti falls.
  4. Will the Thrill (Tim Gravely). Mr. Consistent remains exactly that, point five six three all time, a 2021 runner up finish, and a Sleeper era record of twenty four and eighteen. He drafts Kelce and Garrett Wilson type players early, almost never trades, zero deals across two of his rosters, and just quietly wins with what he drafted. The definition of a lurker.
  5. Good Old American Values (Ryan Bartlett). The perennial best team to never win finally broke through with the 2025 title as Chris Rodriguez Fan Club, on the back of Ja'Marr Chase and Bucky Irving. Fifty two waiver adds in that alias alone, the most aggressive free agency operator in this entire list. Point five six six all time win rate and a 2020 season where he posted the league's top score. The ring finally arrived. More are coming, unfortunately for the rest of us.
  6. Serving Punt (Erica Hallam). A steady point five two one all time and a 2019 runner up finish that still stings a little, in a good way, for the rest of us. Her draft board loves receivers into tight ends, Ekeler and Higgins then Jefferson and McCaffrey then Higgins again, and she works the trade market moderately, six deals as Herbie Fully Loaded. Reliable contender energy, never quite the empire.
  7. Team Sagherian (John S). The other half of the Battle of the Johns, point four seven nine all time, still chasing the ghost of that 2020 nine and four season that somehow lost the title to an eight and five John Vaughan squad. Fifty five waiver adds as Christian McFailMe alone, the busiest wire walker in the league, and ten trades as The Prodigal McCarryme, tops in the field. All that activity and an eighteen and twenty four Sleeper era record. Effort noted. Results pending.
  8. Fitzmagic (Austin Hermann). The 2016 runner up still hunting that elusive first ring, point four six seven all time. Thirty eight waiver adds and zero trades, ever, as if he has taken a vow. He drafts running backs relentlessly, Jacobs, Gibbs, Jeanty, Jacobs again, a running back reunion tour every single April. Seventeen and twenty five in the Sleeper era. The faith is admirable. The results are a slow drip of sadness.
  9. Biggest Dumper (John V). Somehow the most confusing man in league history. He won the 2020 title at eight and five while John Sagherian went nine and four and I posted the league's best record at eleven and two and still went home empty handed. And yet John V sits at point three six one all time, dead last among the vets, and sixteen and twenty six in the Sleeper era. He drafted Amon Ra St. Brown and Nico Collins last spring chasing tight end and receiver value, but light activity, eleven waiver adds, keeps him mostly a bystander. A ring and a basement. The strangest resume in the book.
  10. Matt's moms name (Max Henry). Point four two nine all time, sixteen and twenty six in the Sleeper era, and an extremely quiet transaction sheet, as few as four waiver adds in one season, which is either supreme confidence in his draft or supreme indifference to his roster. He leans tight end and receiver early, Brock Bowers and CeeDee Lamb last spring. The lineup rarely changes. Neither does the record.
  11. Real Eaters (Drew Knight). The lowest active win rate among the veterans at point four oh four, yet somehow the man stole the 2022 crown out of nowhere, the great underdog bite this league has ever seen. Thirteen and twenty nine in the Sleeper era says he has not repeated the magic. His draft record is a running back parade, McCaffrey, Bijan twice, and he keeps a moderate trade habit, six deals as Al Hambra. Dangerous in exactly the way a bad record should never be.
  12. Njigba's in Paris franchise line, the maxhayes97 and related builds (Max Henry, secondary entries). Rolled into the above for record keeping, but worth flagging separately for pure transaction behavior. Low activity across the board, six trades as maxhayes97 against only eleven adds, a boom or bust build strategy that has not yet boomed.

The Field

Watch Elijah Cohen closely this year. A man who has gone from 2016 champion to back to back three win disasters to a Sleeper era record that would make most of this league blush is either building the greatest redemption story we have ever told or setting up the cruelest letdown. Watch Ryan too, because a manager who finally cashed a ring after years of being the best team to never win rarely stops at one. And keep an eye on John Vaughan, if only because a man who won a title at eight and five while owning the worst all time win rate in the league is either due for a correction or is simply allergic to the regular season and immune to the truth. My bold prediction. Somebody not currently in my top five is going to catch fire in November, lose in the semifinal anyway, and get filed permanently under the Ghost of Zach Smith file, the best record with nothing to show for it. History does not just rhyme in this league. It repeats with a vengeance.

The Back Page

Honestly, ranking myself first was not bias, it was arithmetic. Point six nine seven win rate, three rings, a two hundred five point one eight week that still sits alone at the top of the record book. I considered ranking myself second for the appearance of humility and then remembered nobody in this league has ever once accused me of humility, so why start lying to the newsletter now. If there is a rival worth naming, it is Matt R, sitting comfortably at third with a Sleeper era title of his own and a knack for hitting on receivers early. Enjoy third place, Matt. I hear the view from up here is excellent, mostly because I built the ladder myself.
