Preseason Power Rankings: The Empire Strikes Back (Because It Never Left)

Another summer has come and gone, the mock drafts have been mocked, and somewhere out there Zach Smith is still 12 and 1 in 2017, still finished fourth, and still hasn't been allowed to forget it. That's what this league does. It remembers everything. It forgives nothing. And as your humble chronicler, three time champion, and current owner of the best all time winning percentage in league history at point six nine seven, I feel it is my sacred and only mildly self serving duty to tell you exactly where everyone stands before a single snap is played.

The throne remains mine until someone pries it away, but the challengers are circling. JS N My Pants (Dallas) enters with the best Sleeper era win rate in the building and zero rings to show for it, which is its own kind of horror movie. Elijah Cohen is trying to complete the longest redemption arc in league history, from 2016 champion to back to back three win seasons and now back into relevance. And down in the basement, John Vaughan owns a championship and the worst all time win rate among the living, which remains the single strangest sentence in this newsletter's history.

Someone is going to have a signature season. Someone is going to be this year's cautionary tale. Let's get into it.

The Rankings

  1. Straight up Yorkin' it (Matt W). Yes, I put myself first. Yes, you knew I would. But look at the file before you write the angry letter: best all time win rate in league history at point six nine seven, three championships in 2019, 2021, and 2023, and a 2019 season so absurd it still holds the single week scoring record at 205.18. My Sleeper era mark of 25 and 18 might look pedestrian next to some of the young pretenders below, but championships are not won in July. My draft board leans Bijan Robinson and Cooper Kupp type bets, my more recent rooms have chased Amon Ra St. Brown and Puka Nacua, and my transaction sheet shows a manager who works the margins hard, up to 30 adds in a season and five trades in 2023, always hunting an edge without giving away the whole store. The villain edit writes itself. I'm at peace with it.
  2. JS N My Pants (Dallas). The best Sleeper era win rate in the entire league at point six five one, and yet zero titles to show for it, which by my math makes Dallas either extremely unlucky or extremely allergic to Championship Sunday. The draft history is a murderer's row of correct answers, Justin Jefferson and Travis Etienne in 2023, Saquon Barkley and De'Von Achane most recently, and the fact that this year's team made only one waiver move all season tells me a roster that trusts its draft completely. That confidence has to convert eventually. Book it.
  3. Sampson & Dylan lila (Elijah). The founding king of this whole operation, 2016 champion at 12 and 1, has quietly rebuilt himself into a point six two eight Sleeper era operator, second best in the league. The redemption arc from those brutal 3 and 10 and 3 and 11 seasons is essentially complete. Elijah is also the league's most restless trader, nine deals in one season alone, always fishing, and his draft boards skew wide receiver and running back with real swings, from Nick Chubb and A.J. Brown to a 2026 room built around Jonathan Taylor and CeeDee Lamb. The fallen king is back on his feet.
  4. High Risk, High Reward (Matt R). A point six zero five Sleeper era win rate and the reigning process darling after winning it all in 2024 with Olamide Zaccheaus. Matt R drafts like a man allergic to consensus, chasing tight ends and quarterbacks early in some years, then pivoting hard to Ja'Marr Chase and Amon Ra St. Brown in others, and his 2026 room is stacked around Christian McCaffrey and Ashton Jeanty. Light on trades, moderate on waivers, heavy on conviction. The gap between him and me is thinner than I would like to admit out loud.
  5. Will the Thrill (Tim). Mr. Consistent has the receipts to prove it, a 2021 runner up finish and a Sleeper era win rate of point five five eight that has never wildly swung in either direction. Tim's rooms lean receiver heavy, Travis Kelce and Garrett Wilson, Tyreek Hill and Chris Olave, and this year he's leaning into a Jahmyr Gibbs and Jeremiyah Love backfield bet. He barely touches the trade market, zero deals in multiple seasons, preferring to win with what he drafted. Steady. Dangerous. Perpetually underestimated.
  6. Hawk Tua on my Penix ✝️ (Ryan). Ryan is the league's great almost, a 2020 top scorer with 1801.68 points and still no pre Sleeper ring, until last year finally broke through with the 2025 title behind Chris Rodriguez Fan Club. That team was also the most active roster in the league by far, a staggering 52 waiver adds and seven trades in the championship season alone. This year's build sits at a nearly frozen two waiver adds, which either means total faith in the draft or a manager resting on his laurels. History says bet on Ryan finding one more gear.
  7. Serving Punt ✨ (Erica). A 2019 runner up and a career point five two one all time win rate, Erica has always been the quiet contender nobody wants to face in November. Her draft rooms have repeatedly bet big on true alphas, Austin Ekeler and Justin Jefferson, Christian McCaffrey and Tee Higgins, and this year she's built around James Cook and Drake London. A moderate but real trader, six deals in one season, she chases wide receivers and tight ends and rarely panics. The floor is high here.
  8. How bout them Cowboys (John S). John Sagherian is forever linked to the Battle of the Johns, a 2020 finals loss at 9 and 4 to a John V team that finished 8 and 5, which remains one of the great injustices in the record book. His Sleeper era win rate sits at point four four two, but his transaction habits tell a story of a manager who never stops working, 55 adds in one season, ten trades in another. That kind of desperation to improve eventually pays off. This might be the year the Johns saga tilts back his way.
  9. Hampton Water (Austin). The 2016 runner up is still hunting his first title a full decade later, and his all time win rate of point four six seven reflects a career of near misses. Austin's draft tendency is the most quarterback curious in the league, having actually spent early picks on Patrick Mahomes, alongside a steady diet of running backs, Jahmyr Gibbs, Ashton Jeanty, and now Omarion Hampton. He is also the most waiver active manager in the sample with zero trades, a true grinder who builds from the wire rather than the trade block. Somewhere in there is a breakout waiting to happen.
  10. Matt's moms name (Max). Max's point four two nine all time win rate places him firmly in the league's lower half, but his draft board has quietly gotten sharper, CeeDee Lamb and Brock Bowers last year, Bijan Robinson and Trey McBride this year. The strange part is his transaction sheet, only four waiver adds and three trades in his most recent tracked season, one of the quietest rosters in the league. Whether that is discipline or inattention will decide his season.
  11. I still own you (John V). The most fascinating case file in the league. John Vaughan won the 2020 championship at 8 and 5 while posting the worst all time win rate among active managers at point three six one. A ring and a basement, together forever. His most recent draft capital went to Amon Ra St. Brown and Nico Collins, then Ja'Marr Chase and Brock Bowers this year, which is at least promising on paper. The record book says regression is common. The trophy case says never count him out completely.
  12. Real Eaters (Drew). Drew Knight owns the lowest active win rate among the true veterans in the all time table at point four zero four, which makes his 2022 championship run all the more delightfully improbable, the ultimate underdog bite. His 2021 season also produced the single highest points total in league history at 1878.60, so the ceiling is undeniably real even when the floor gives way. This year's draft brought Justin Jefferson and Kenneth Walker, modest activity, low trade volume. The lowest average finish, but never rule out the team that already shocked everybody once.

The Field

Watch Dallas closely this year. A point six five one win rate with no hardware is either the setup for a legendary breakthrough or the setup for a legendary meltdown, and this league does not do things quietly. Watch Elijah too, because a founding champion clawing back from back to back three win seasons into the top tier of the power rankings is the kind of story that makes newsletters like this one worth writing. And do not sleep on John Sagherian, whose transaction sheet reads like a man who refuses to accept his Battle of the Johns defeat as final. My bold prediction: this is the year the gap between win rate and championships finally closes for somebody near the top of this list, and I do not think it is going to be me, mostly because I already have three and the math says it is someone else's turn. Probably.

The Back Page

I ranked myself first. I stand by it. Best all time win rate in recorded league history, three championships, a single week scoring record that has stood since 2019, and a roster built this year around Puka Nacua and Kyren Williams that I would happily stack against anyone in this newsletter. Is it a little suspicious that the guy writing the power rankings put himself on top? Sure. Is it also just factually accurate? Also sure. Both things can be true, and frankly that is the whole appeal of being me. If I have to name a rival, it is Dallas. Best Sleeper era win rate in the league, sharp drafting, and yet zero rings, which means somewhere out there Dallas is looking at my three championships the way the rest of the league looks at Zach Smith's 2017 season, all that talent and nothing to show for it. Keep grinding over there. I'll be here, adding a fourth banner to the rafters.
