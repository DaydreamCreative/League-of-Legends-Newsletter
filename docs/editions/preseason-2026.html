The Preseason Power Rankings: Who's Really Champion Until Somebody Stops Me

Another offseason has come and gone, which means it is once again time for me, Matthew Waters, three time champion (2019, 2021, 2023) and the man with the best all time win percentage in the history of this league at point six nine seven, to sit down and tell you exactly where you stand. This is not bragging. This is journalism. The numbers simply happen to agree with me.

The state of the league heading into 2026 is this: I am still the villain everyone claims they are gunning for, yet somehow I am also the only manager with three rings, which tells you how that has been going. Ryan Bartlett enters as the defending Sleeper era champion after finally cashing in on years of scoring points nobody could beat him in the games that mattered, and Matt R has quietly built the most efficient winning percentage of the Sleeper era without anybody noticing, which is exactly how a heist works. Meanwhile the basement remains crowded. Drew Knight owns the lowest all time win rate among the veterans at point four oh four, John Vaughan somehow won a title in 2020 and still sits dead last at point three six one, and Elijah Cohen is still trying to climb out of the crater he dug himself in 2020 and 2021 after his glorious 2016 coronation.

So who is the team to beat? Still me, and I will die on this hill. Who is out for redemption? Elijah, Austin, and honestly at this point John Vaughan, who has a ring nobody respects because his career winning percentage looks like a typo.

The Rankings

  1. Straight up Yorkin' it (Matt W) - Best all time win percentage in league history at point six nine seven, three championships (2019, 2021, 2023), and a Sleeper era record of twenty five and seventeen that most managers would frame on their wall. My draft process is simple and correct: hammer running backs and receivers early, this year Puka Nacua and Kyren Williams in the first two rounds, then work the wire with real purpose, twenty seven adds this era and five trades, never scared to make a move. I am ranked first because I have earned it, and also because I am writing this newsletter, but mostly because I have earned it.
  2. Sampson & Dylen-lila (Elijah) - The fallen founding king is rising. Elijah won it all in 2016 at twelve and one, cratered horribly in 2020 and 2021, and has since quietly rebuilt to a point six one nine Sleeper era win rate, tied for the second best active mark in the league. He has been aggressive across rosters over the years, nine trades on one build alone, though this year's Sagherian squad has gone eerily quiet on the wire with only three adds. The redemption arc is real. Just stop trading your good players to other people in the top five.
  3. Max's moms name (Matt R) - Point six one nine in the Sleeper era and a point five seven one mark across all time, plus the 2024 championship with Olamide Zaccheaus. Matt R drafts receivers early and often, Ja'Marr Chase, Malik Nabers, and this year Christian McCaffrey and Ashton Jeanty back to back, and backs it up with twenty six waiver adds and seven trades, a manager who is never satisfied with his roster as constructed. Quietly one of the scariest teams in the league.
  4. JS N My Pants (Dallas) - The single best Sleeper era win rate in the league at point six four three, twenty seven and fifteen, and yet zero rings to show for it, which makes Dallas this era's Ryan Bartlett. Consistently reaches receiver and running back early, Justin Jefferson back in 2023 and now Saquon Barkley and De'Von Achane this year, and keeps a moderate transaction pace. The record says contender. The trophy case says otherwise. That gap is going to close eventually, and I would rather it not be this year.
  5. Will the Thrill (Tim) - Mr. Consistent lives up to the name, point five six three all time and point five seven one in the Sleeper era, a 2021 runner up finish, and a habit of drafting tight ends and receivers at the top, Travis Kelce back in the day and now Jahmyr Gibbs and Jeremiyah Love. Tim does not trade much and does not overspend the wire, ten adds one year, twenty five another, zero trades in both. Steady, dependable, always lurking around the top of the standings without ever quite grabbing the headline.
  6. Hawk Tua on my Penix (Ryan) - The reigning Sleeper era champion via the Chris Rodriguez Fan Club roster, and a manager with big scoring history dating back to a league record 186.92 in a single week back in 2021 plus the best record that season at ten and four. Ryan chases receivers and running backs at the top of drafts and has run rosters anywhere from a sleepy one add all the way up to fifty two adds and seven trades depending on the year. The ring finally happened. Now we find out if he can defend it, because right now this particular roster has gone almost silent on the wire.
  7. Serving Punt (Erica) - A 2019 runner up finish and a point five two one all time win rate, Erica has been a legitimate contender for a long time without breaking through. Draft tendencies lean receiver heavy with tight end mixed in, Christian McCaffrey and Tee Higgins headlining recent classes, and a moderate transaction approach, twenty one adds, six trades. The talent is there. The ring is not. That sentence describes half this league, honestly.
  8. Team Sagherian (John S) - The other half of the Battle of the Johns, a 2020 runner up at nine and four who still somehow lost the title to a team with a losing... well, an eight and five record. All time win rate sits at point four seven nine. Draft approach favors running backs and receivers, Saquon Barkley and Amon-Ra St. Brown among the picks, and his rosters have ranged from wildly active, fifty five adds and ten trades in peak years, to nearly dormant this year at three adds. Somewhere between those extremes is a team that could contend again.
  9. Hampton Water (Austin) - The 2016 runner up still hunting his first ring a decade later. All time win rate of point four six seven and a Sleeper era mark of point four oh five suggest the hunt continues. Austin is famously loyal to the quarterback and running back well early in drafts, Patrick Mahomes, Josh Jacobs twice over, and now Omarion Hampton, and he is the rare manager who never trades, zero deals across the Sleeper era, while still working the wire hard, thirty eight adds one season alone. Stubborn, active, ringless. A frustrating combination.
  10. I still own you (John V) - Living proof that championships and win rates do not always agree. John V won the whole thing in 2020 at eight and five and yet owns the worst all time win percentage among active managers at point three six one, plus a middling point three eight one in the Sleeper era. Chases receivers and tight ends early, Ja'Marr Chase and Brock Bowers this cycle, and keeps his transaction ledger relatively quiet. The ring buys him a lot of grace. It does not buy him a higher seed here.
  11. Matt's moms name (Max) - A point four two nine all time win rate and point three eight one in the Sleeper era put Max near the bottom, though he has shown real spine at the top of drafts, CeeDee Lamb, Brock Bowers, and now Bijan Robinson and Trey McBride in back to back top picks. The problem is everything after the draft. Some years just four waiver adds, barely touching the wire, which in this league is basically forfeiting free improvement. Fix that and the record follows.
  12. Real Eaters (Drew) - Drew owns the 2022 championship banner, which by rule I have to mention, but his all time win rate of point four oh four is the lowest among the league's active veterans, and his Sleeper era mark of point three one oh, thirteen and twenty nine, is dead last in the room. Draft approach favors receivers and backs, Christian McCaffrey and now Justin Jefferson, but the transaction activity has been comparatively light. The ring proves he can get hot. The rest of the resume says he needs to get hot again, soon.

The Field

Keep an eye on Matt R's operation, a team built on early receiver capital and constant tinkering that has already delivered one championship and might be the league's quietest juggernaut. Dallas remains the great regular season mirage, best Sleeper era win rate in the building and nothing to show for it, which either ends in a parade or another year of everyone else sleeping soundly. On the way down, watch Max and John Vaughan, two rosters that could use either more waiver activity or, in John V's case, some acknowledgment that a five year old ring does not pay this year's bills.

Bold prediction: Elijah completes the full redemption arc from 2016 champion to 2020 and 2021 laughingstock to 2026 contender, and somebody not named Matt Waters finally learns what it feels like to hoist the trophy in back to back seasons, because Ryan is not going away quietly.

The Back Page

Honestly, if I am grading myself with a straight face, I put me at number one, and I have the receipts to back it up: best all time win percentage in league history, three championships, and a Sleeper era record that only Dallas can claim to beat over these recent years. Is it a little suspicious that the guy writing the power rankings ranked himself first? Sure. Is it also just objectively correct? Also sure. Those two things can coexist, and in this newsletter, they always will.

My one real gripe heading into the season is with Dallas Dickinson, who has the audacity to own the best Sleeper era win rate in the entire league, point six four three, and zero rings to justify the swagger that comes with it. Dallas, I have three trophies and a losing week fifteen from 2019 that still outscores your whole roster some Sundays. Put up or keep polishing that gaudy regular season record for the rest of us to admire from the podium.
