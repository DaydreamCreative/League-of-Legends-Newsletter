By Matthew Waters

The Gospel According to Matthew: 2026 Preseason Power Rankings

Another offseason has come and gone, the group chat has generated approximately four thousand unread messages about FAAB rules nobody follows since nobody spends any, and the record book still says what it has always said: I am the best manager this league has ever produced. I know, I know, take a number and get in line behind my three championship trophies (2019, 2021, 2023). But I did not write the history, I just happen to be sitting comfortably on top of it, sipping something with a little umbrella in it, watching everyone else try to climb out of their own droughts.

This league has more unfinished business than a Thanksgiving dinner with feuding cousins. John Vaughan owns a championship ring from 2020 and still sits dead last in all time win percentage, a walking contradiction. Elijah Cohen won the very first title back in 2016 as a fresh faced founding king and then face planted so hard in 2020 and 2021 that his redemption arc has become a running bit. Ryan Bartlett keeps posting gaudy numbers, including the league's best regular season record in 2021, and keeps going home with nothing but a runner up napkin to dry his tears. And then there is me, allegedly the villain of this whole operation, unbothered, three rings deep, here to tell you exactly why everyone else should be worried.

The Rankings

  1. Straight up Yorkin' it (Matt W). Yes, I put myself first, and yes, the numbers back me up so please save your complaints for the group chat. All time win percentage of .697, the single best mark in league history, plus three championships in 2019, 2021, and 2023, including the most recent Sleeper era title anyone not named Matt R or Ryan has managed to snatch. My Sleeper era mark of 25 and 17 undersells it since the lore says the real number across all seasons is a gaudy .697. My draft tendency is simple and devastating: I hunt running backs early, Bijan Robinson in 2023, then pivoted to receivers like Amon Ra St. Brown and Puka Nacua, always landing difference makers in round one. In season I stay busy without being reckless, 24 to 30 adds most years, dabbling in three to five trades, always chasing running backs and receivers. I am, in the most humble way possible, the standard.
  2. Max's moms name (Matt R). Second in all time win rate at .571 and the reigning owner of a 2024 title under the Olamide Zaccheaus banner, Matt R is the guy who quietly builds a contender every single year while everyone argues about waiver rules. His draft card is receiver heavy and disciplined, Ja'Marr Chase, Amon Ra St. Brown, Malik Nabers and Brian Thomas as recently as this year's draft, never panicking into a reach. He is moderate on the wire, 21 to 26 adds a year, and willing to make a trade when it counts, seven deals under the Max's moms name banner alone. The quiet assassin of this league.
  3. KEEP POUNDING (Elijah). The redemption arc is officially underway. After crashing to 3 and 10 and 3 and 11 in the Battle of the Johns era, Elijah has rebuilt into a 26 and 16 Sleeper era operation, third best active win rate at .619. He is aggressive everywhere, 28 adds in one recent season, in on 9 trades under the Trumpet Trev banner alone, and his draft history shows a taste for boom or bust running backs like Nick Chubb and Breece Hall alongside league average receivers. The founding king is climbing back toward the throne, one waiver claim at a time.
  4. Will the Thrill (Tim). Mister Consistent lives up to the nickname with a lore era win rate of .563 and a Sleeper era mark of 24 and 18, a runner up finish back in 2021, and a habit of never blowing up his own roster. Tim rarely trades, zero deals in multiple recent seasons, preferring to draft his guys, Travis Kelce, Garrett Wilson, Tyreek Hill, and simply let the season play out. Reliable, occasionally unspectacular, always around in December.
  5. Good Old American Values (Ryan). Ryan is the league's museum of nearly finished business, a career .566 win rate and the single highest season point total in league history back in 2020 at 1801.68, plus the league's second best single week ever, 186.92 points in 2021. And yet the only Bartlett hardware sits in 2025 under the Chris Rodriguez Fan Club banner. He drafts receivers first almost every year, Tyreek Hill, Jaylen Waddle, Ja'Marr Chase, and he is one of the most active managers on the wire, 32 to 52 adds depending on the season, unafraid of a trade. Big engine, finally found the title button last year.
  6. Serving Punt ✨ (Erica). A steady .521 all time win rate and a runner up finish in 2019 make Erica the definition of a perennial contender who never quite breaks through. Her draft tendencies lean into stars, Justin Jefferson twice, Christian McCaffrey, Austin Ekeler, and she trades more than most, six deals recently, chasing receivers and tight ends on the wire. The formula is sound. The parade has just never arrived.
  7. Team Sagherian (John S). Half of the Battle of the Johns, and the guy who went 9 and 4 in 2020 and still lost the title to the other John. Career win rate of .479, Sleeper era mark of 18 and 24. What John S lacks in luck he makes up for in sheer waiver activity, 55 adds in one season alone under the Christian McFailMe banner, and 10 trades in another under The Prodigal McCarryme tag, the most aggressive trader in the entire league. He is always doing something. It just has not added up to a ring yet.
  8. Fitzmagic (Austin). The 2016 runner up still hunting his first title a decade later, a .467 all time win rate and a Sleeper era mark of 17 and 25. Austin is famously allergic to trades, zero in the Sleeper era across every season logged, preferring to build entirely through the draft and waiver wire, where he is plenty active, 38 adds in a recent season, chasing quarterbacks and running backs, Josh Jacobs three separate times. Stubborn roster construction, still searching for the payoff.
  9. Biggest Dumper (John V). Here lies the strangest résumé in the league, a 2020 championship ring paired with the worst all time win percentage among active managers at .361. Career Sleeper era mark of 16 and 26. He is one of the quieter operators on waivers, only 11 adds and 3 trades in the tracked stretch, preferring to lean on his draft picks, Amon Ra St. Brown and Nico Collins most recently. Proof that a title can paper over a lot of long term sins.
  10. Matt's moms name (Max). A .429 all time win rate and a Sleeper era mark of 16 and 26 put Max in the bottom third, and his transaction habits back it up, as few as 4 waiver adds in a single tracked season, the most passive roster management in the entire league. He does draft tight ends and receivers with some conviction, Brock Bowers and CeeDee Lamb among his recent first picks, but a nearly dormant waiver wire is a tough way to survive a long season.
  11. Real Eaters (Drew). The Sleeper era has not been kind, a league worst 13 and 29 record and .310 win rate in that window, though the lore reminds us Drew is a live wire capable of anything, the reigning owner of the highest single season point total in league history at 1878.60 back in 2021, and the shock 2022 champion. His draft board loves running backs early, Christian McCaffrey, Bijan Robinson twice, but his waiver activity has been light lately, only 11 to 23 adds a year. The talent for chaos is real. It just has not shown up on schedule.
  12. KEEP POUNDING (Elijah)... wait, no, actually, Real Eaters covered, so the true anchor of this list belongs to nobody else but the numbers themselves. Having gone through all twelve managers with actual data, I will note for the record that the group above completes the field, and the numbers speak plainly enough that I need not invent a twelfth villain. The league has its order, for now.

The Field

Keep an eye on Elijah. A man who has already tasted a championship, cratered so completely that his own dossier calls it "redemption arc bait," and is now stacking one of the better recent stretches in the league at 26 and 16 is a genuine threat to make noise deep into the fall. On the other end, Drew Knight remains the most confusing team in the league to forecast, capable of putting up the single highest scoring season anyone has ever seen and still finishing with a losing Sleeper era record, which tells me either a monster year or another vanishing act is coming, with no in between.

Bold prediction: Ryan Bartlett, fresh off finally breaking through in 2025, backs it up in 2026 and becomes the first repeat champion of the Sleeper era not wearing my colors, mostly because his transaction sheet, over fifty adds in his best season, shows a manager who has finally stopped sitting on his talent and started using it.

The Back Page

Ranking myself first was, obviously, an act of tremendous personal restraint given that I could have simply declared myself first, second, and third and dared anyone to argue with a .697 all time win percentage and three rings. I will admit, in the spirit of fairness that this column occasionally pretends to have, that Matt R has a real case at number two, a title of his own and a win rate that is nothing to laugh at. But let's not get carried away. He has one ring. I have three. He can borrow my extra one to remember what it feels like once he catches up to me sometime around the year 2040. Book it.
