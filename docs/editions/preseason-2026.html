The Preseason Power Rankings: Ten Years of Receipts and One Insufferable Champion

Another summer, another round of everyone in this league lying to themselves about their roster while I sit here with three rings, the best all time winning percentage in the ten year history of this operation, and absolutely nothing left to prove. That is not bragging, that is the record book talking. I just happen to be the one holding the microphone this week, which, if you have been paying attention since 2016, is exactly how this always goes.

But history is not just my highlight reel. Elijah Cohen won the very first championship in 2016 and has spent most of the years since digging out of the hole he built for himself, including a brutal three win campaign in 2021. John Vaughan owns a ring from 2020 and somehow still owns the worst winning percentage among all active managers, a stat so delicious I want it tattooed on the league trophy. Drew Knight snuck away with the 2022 crown despite a losing career record, proof this league occasionally rewards chaos over quality. And Ryan Bartlett keeps racking up gaudy numbers and monster weeks without ever quite closing the deal until last year's title finally silenced some of that noise.

The Rankings

  1. Straight up Yorkin' it (Matt W). Yes, I put myself first. Sue me. Best all time winning percentage in league history at point six nine seven, three championships in 2019, 2021, and 2023, and a Sleeper era record of twenty five and seventeen that still ranks fourth in modern form despite my recent teams being younger and rowdier than my dynasty rosters. My draft board loves running backs early, Bijan Robinson in 2023, then a De'Von Achane pivot, then Puka Nacua twice in a row, which tells you I chase upside without fear. In season I am moderately active, twenty seven adds combined across my two most recent builds and five trades on my title team alone, because I am not afraid to shake a roster that is already winning. The villain edit writes itself and I have made peace with it.
  2. JS N My Pants (Dallas). The best Sleeper era winning percentage in the entire league at point six four three, twenty seven and fifteen, and he did it as a total waiver ghost, a single add and zero trades on this particular roster. That is either supreme confidence in his draft or the laziest championship caliber season I have ever covered. His draft history is a murderer's row of true value picks, Justin Jefferson, Saquon Barkley, De'Von Achane, always chasing wide receiver and running back capital early. All time win rate of point five two three keeps him from cracking the very top, but the recent trajectory is terrifying.
  3. Sampson & Dylan-lila (Elijah). The founding king of 2016 has clawed his way back into relevance, posting a point six one nine Sleeper era mark, second best in the league right now. His all time number sits at a respectable point five four one once you smooth out the disasters of 2020 and 2021. He is one of the most trade happy managers in the league, nine trades on one roster alone, and he leans wide receiver and running back early while occasionally sneaking a tight end or quarterback swing. The redemption arc is officially back on.
  4. High Risk, High Reward (Matt R). Point six one nine in the Sleeper era, a point five seven one all time mark, and a 2024 championship banner hanging in his garage. His draft habits skew toward stars at receiver and running back, Ja'Marr Chase, Jonathan Taylor, this year a McCaffrey and Jeanty pairing that is either genius or a hospital waiting room. He chases tight end and quarterback on waivers more than most, an unusual taste that has quietly worked.
  5. Will the Thrill (Tim). Mr. Consistent remains consistent, point five six three in the Sleeper era and point five six three all time, a runner up finish back in 2021 and never a disaster season since. He barely touches waivers, ten adds and zero trades in one recent year, a set it and forget it approach that somehow keeps producing. Drafts wide receiver and running back early, and this year doubled down on backfield juice with Gibbs and Jeremiyah Love.
  6. Hawk Tua on my Penix ✝️ (Ryan). The defending champion, fresh off the 2025 title, and owner of the highest single week score in league history, one hundred eighty six point nine two back in 2020. All time win rate of point five six six, Sleeper era at an even point five two four. He has run everything from a fifty two add fire sale year to a one add hibernation year, proof he adapts to whatever a season demands. The former best team to never win now has a ring, and that changes the whole conversation.
  7. Serving Punt ✨ (Erica). The 2019 runner up remains a steady, if unspectacular, presence, point five two four in the Sleeper era and point five two one all time. Moderate waiver activity, moderate trade activity, a fondness for wide receivers and tight ends that fits her patient team building style. Not scary yet, but never quite an easy week either.
  8. How bout them Cowboys (John S). Half of the Battle of the Johns, John S ran into John V in the 2020 final and has been chasing that ghost ever since, point four seven nine all time and point four two nine in the Sleeper era. His transaction habits swing wildly, from a fifty five add fire drill in one season to a three add nothing burger the next, chasing running backs and receivers whenever the mood strikes.
  9. Hampton Water (Austin). The 2016 runner up still hunting his first ring, point four six seven all time and point four zero five in the Sleeper era. He is one of the most draft focused, transaction shy managers in the league in some years, though other builds show him at thirty eight adds and heavy on quarterback and running back targets. This year's Omarion Hampton and Derrick Henry pairing is a bold, very on brand swing for a team named after his own sparkling rose habit.
  10. I still own you (John V). Won the whole thing in 2020 at eight and five and has been living in the basement of the standings ever since, worst all time winning percentage among active managers at point three six one. Sleeper era mark of point three eight one is not much better. Chases receivers hard, this year grabbing Ja'Marr Chase and Brock Bowers in the first two rounds, but a ring is a ring and nobody can take that engraving off the trophy.
  11. Matt's moms name (Max). Point four two nine all time, point three eight one in the Sleeper era, and a genuinely bizarre transaction profile, four adds and three trades in one recent season, almost no waiver activity but plenty of horse trading. Chases tight ends more than almost anyone in the league, Brock Bowers and Trey McBride both landed on his roster in back to back years.
  12. Real Eaters (Drew). The lowest active win rate among the true veterans at point four zero four all time, and a rough point three one zero in the Sleeper era, thirteen and twenty nine. And yet, this is the same manager who stole the 2022 championship outright, so nobody sleeps on Drew Knight completely. Chases wide receivers and running backs early, and remains capable of biting at exactly the wrong moment for whoever is favored.

The Field

Watch Dallas closely this year. A point six four three Sleeper era winning percentage built almost entirely without touching the waiver wire is either a mirage or a genuinely elite roster construction habit, and I suspect it is the latter. Elijah's trade heavy, waiver hungry approach finally has box scores to match the ambition, which makes the fallen founding king dangerous again after nearly a decade in the wilderness. On the other side, keep an eye on John Vaughan and Max Henry, both sitting at the bottom of the all time chart with transaction habits that swing from totally dormant to frantic, the kind of inconsistency that either means a breakout is coming or another long winter.

Bold prediction: Ryan Bartlett becomes the first back to back champion of the Sleeper era, because a manager who already owns the league's most explosive single week ever and finally has a ring in the trophy case is the most dangerous kind of dangerous, a talented team with something to prove all over again.

The Back Page

Honestly, ranking myself first was the only defensible outcome given three championships and the best winning percentage this league has ever recorded, but I am fully aware how that sentence reads coming from the guy writing the newsletter. Call it a conflict of interest, call it the villain arc, call it simply respecting the numbers. Either way, my real concern heading into this season is Dallas Dickinson, a Sleeper era winning percentage that is currently the best in the building and a draft history stacked with names I would have taken myself. Enjoy the top of the rankings while it lasts, Dallas. I have three rings that say I know how to handle company up here.
