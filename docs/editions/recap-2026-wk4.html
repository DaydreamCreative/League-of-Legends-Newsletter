By Matthew Waters

Week Four: The Dynasty Takes an L, and the League Keeps Right on Spinning

I'll say this plainly because that's the kind of stand up guy I am: Straight up Yorkin' it got beat this week, and I'm not going to bury the lede under six paragraphs of spin before I admit it. But friends, while my own ship took on water, the real story of Week Four is that Hampton Water sailed to four and oh, Elijah Cohen's redemption tour hit a speed bump, and Max Henry's squad rocketed up the standings like it owed somebody money. Grab a chair. There's a lot to get into, and shockingly, not all of it is about me. Shockingly.

The Recaps

JS N My Pants 141.52, Straight up Yorkin' it 114.28

Let the record show I am a three time champion and the winningest manager in recorded league history, and let the record also show that Tetairoa McMillan hung 37.2 points on me like he owed me nothing and I owed him an apology. Dallas Dickinson's squad got monster lines from McMillan and Chuba Hubbard (25.4) to go with a sturdy 22.72 from Joe Burrow, and that was enough cushion even with Saquon Barkley cratering to a grim 0.5. On my side, Kyren Williams (31.7) and Javonte Williams (28.8) both went off, Puka Nacua chipped in 23.2, and I still lost by over 27 points, which tells you something about Davante Adams no showing at 5.2 and me leaving Tyler Shough's 15.94 rotting on my bench behind Matthew Stafford's quiet 9.68. The series still belongs to Dallas 3 to 1, and I'll admit through gritted teeth that it stings. Championship pedigree doesn't matter when your quarterback room forgets to show up.

How bout them Cowboys 85.42, Hawk Tua on my Penix ✝️ 71.7

John Sagherian ground this one out behind Brock Purdy's 19.62 and a nice 20.3 from Alvin Kamara, surviving a genuine zero from Ladd McConkey and a near zero from Parker Washington (1.5). Ryan Bartlett got a solid 17.0 from Patrick Mahomes and a sneaky 14.4 from Devaughn Vele, but this game turned into an autopsy of the bench. Ryan left Brian Robinson (25.2) and Emanuel Wilson (24.5) parked on his sideline while David Montgomery managed just 4.3 and Bucky Irving limped to 6.1. That's nearly 50 points of running back sitting on the bench while the starters combined for barely over 10. John S didn't need to be perfect, he just needed Ryan's lineup card to keep misfiring, and it obliged.

Hampton Water 103.66, Serving Punt ✨ 95.86

Austin Hermann is four and oh now and I genuinely don't know what to do with that information except report it. Bryce Young quietly dropped 21.46, George Kittle looked every bit the matchup nightmare at 15.0, and Austin got there despite leaving Drake Maye's 23.16 on the bench, which is the kind of problem you're allowed to have when you're undefeated. Erica Hallam fought hard, Zay Flowers was excellent at 21.8 and James Cook chipped in 15.3, but Rashee Rice's zero and a pedestrian 8.56 from Justin Herbert left too big a hole to climb out of. Austin now leads this series 3 to 2 historically, but on the day it was simply the deeper team winning a track meet.

Matt's moms name 133.58, Sampson & Dylan-lila 114.82

Max Henry put together the highest score of the week not already discussed and did it with a committee approach, Bijan Robinson (27.2), Nico Collins (27.3), and Kyle Monangai (27.5) all going off in the same afternoon like a three car pileup of fantasy production. C.J. Stroud added a tidy 23.08. Elijah Cohen countered with a massive CeeDee Lamb day (32.8) and solid floor from Jonathan Taylor (22.2), but Jalen Hurts stayed modest at 12.52 and Braelon Allen never got going at 7.7. Elijah's bench featured a juicy 20.8 from Romeo Doubs sitting unused, the kind of thing that turns a competitive loss into a frustrating one. Max climbs to 4 and 2 in this head to head and, more importantly for him, vaults six spots in the standings. That's not a typo. Six.

High Risk, High Reward 104.28, Real Eaters 96.68

Matt R keeps the historical stranglehold alive, now 6 and 0 against Drew Knight all time, and did it this week on the strength of Ashton Jeanty (15.6), Rhamondre Stevenson (16.9), and Malik Nabers (20.2) while Trevor Lawrence played mistake free with 13.08. Drew got a monstrous 30.4 out of Kenneth Walker, arguably the single best running back performance of the week for anybody, but Lamar Jackson only managed 18.88 and too many complementary pieces came up small, Chris Godwin at 6.1 and Dalton Schultz at a near invisible 1.9. Matt R also left real points on his own bench, Jared Goff's 20.48 sat the whole game, but when you're already winning by single digits, apparently you can afford a mistake like that. Drew cannot.

I still own you 86.78, Will the Thrill 72.02

John Vaughan evened this rivalry back up at two and two with a balanced attack, Quinshon Judkins (18.6), Brock Bowers (17.6), and DK Metcalf (14.0) all pulling weight even as Ja'Marr Chase face planted to 4.2. Tim Gravely got a fine floor game from Josh Allen (17.52) and Jahmyr Gibbs (15.7), but DJ Moore and Kalif Raymond combined for a grand total of 3.8 points, which is the kind of wide receiver room malfunction that loses you games outright. Tim's bench had Malachi Fields sitting on 12.2 while Travis Kelce started and produced a ghostly 2.5. That swap alone was nearly a ten point swing in a game decided by less than fifteen.

Standings and Movers

Hampton Water sits alone at the top at four and oh, the only unbeaten left standing, and climbed a spot doing it. Sampson & Dylan-lila still leads the league in total points but slipped to third and dropped a spot after the loss to Max. JS N My Pants rockets up two spots to third behind Dallas's big week. The headline mover, though, is Matt's moms name, up six spots into fourth after that outburst from Max's running backs and receivers.

On the other end, Will the Thrill falls three spots and now sits one and three, and my own Straight up Yorkin' it tumbles three spots as well after the loss to Dallas, which I'm contractually obligated to mention hurts my feelings more than my record. Hawk Tua on my Penix ✝️ also drops three spots, still searching for answers after leaving that pile of bench points unused against John S. Serving Punt slides three spots too, proof that even a near ninety six point outing isn't safe in a league scoring like this one.

Looking Ahead

Week Five brings me a date with Serving Punt in a game between two teams both sitting at two and two, which should be a nice tune up before I remember how to win again. Elsewhere, Hampton Water's unbeaten run gets tested against a hungry High Risk, High Reward squad, the Battle of the Johns flavor runs through John V hosting Elijah Cohen, and Ryan Bartlett gets a crack at the surging Dallas Dickinson squad that just humbled me personally. I'll be watching all of it, mostly out of spite.

The Back Page

Yes, I lost. Yes, Tetairoa McMillan treated my secondary like an all you can eat buffet. But let's not lose perspective here, I am still the winningest manager in the history of this league, a three time champion, and the kind of competitor who takes an L in stride before immediately plotting the comeback. Two and two is simply where legends pause for dramatic effect before the real surge begins. Write that down. I'll be back to collecting rings and bending this newsletter to my own glory by the time Week Five wraps. Until then, Dallas, enjoy it while it lasts.
