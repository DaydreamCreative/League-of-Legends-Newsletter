2026 Draft RecapSeptember 09, 2026
By Matthew Waters

Twelve Rooms, Twelve Egos, One Draft Board on Fire

I sat through every pick of this thing with a legal pad and a growing headache, and here is the plain truth: there was no consensus strategy in this league this year. Some rooms zigged into running backs like it was still 2015, some rooms zagged into wide receiver depth until their bench looked like a WR2 factory outlet, and at least one room forgot the tight end position exists until round eleven. I am grading this with as straight a face as I can manage, my own team included, because the record book does not care about my feelings and neither should you.

Team by Team

Hampton Water (Austin) (B minus)

Austin's strength is a legitimate running back one two punch in Omarion Hampton and Derrick Henry, the kind of foundation that wins weeks by itself. The weakness is a receiver room that gets thin fast after George Pickens and Jaylen Waddle, and a bizarre double dip at quarterback with Drake Maye and Jaxson Dart that burned two mid to late picks he probably needed elsewhere. The highlight is George Kittle falling to round eight, an absolute gift at tight end. The head scratcher is taking Chris Rodriguez in round nine when the receiver depth was already screaming for help. Verdict: a power run game looking for a passing attack to match it.

JS N My Pants (Dallas) (A minus)

Dallas built the deepest, most balanced roster in the league, and it shows everywhere from Saquon Barkley and De'Von Achane at the top to Alvin Kamara as a round fourteen luxury bench piece. The lone weakness is a slight lack of true difference making upside at receiver beyond Tee Higgins and Mike Evans. The highlight is Travis Hunter in round thirteen, pure value at a scarce defensive back slot nobody else even drafted. The questionable pick is Bo Nix in round twelve as a third quarterback body when a swing at receiver depth was still on the board. Verdict: no glaring holes, championship shaped roster.

Real Eaters (Drew) (B plus)

Drew's strength is a receiver corps seven deep, anchored by Justin Jefferson and reinforced all the way down to Keenan Allen in round thirteen. The weakness is a running back room that gets shaky in the middle rounds once you get past Kenneth Walker and Travis Etienne. The highlight is Lamar Jackson sliding to round three, a legitimate top shelf quarterback at a discount. The questionable pick is Mike Washington in round ten, a running back flier with a crowded backfield in front of him. Verdict: Drew finally drafted like a man trying to shake the worst all time win percentage in the league.

Sampson & Dylan-lila (Elijah) (B)

Elijah's strength is a loaded receiver room built around Jonathan Taylor's backfield partner CeeDee Lamb, plus Chris Olave and Garrett Wilson giving him three legitimate starters. The weakness is a running back room that is deep in bodies but light on proven bell cow certainty after Taylor. The highlight is Jonathan Taylor at pick one overall, still the safest bet in the format. The questionable pick is stacking Braelon Allen and Dylan Sampson in the final rounds, dart throws in a backfield committee that may not clear. Verdict: the founding king building a real contender again.

Serving Punt ✨ (Erica) (B minus)

Erica's strength is a smart, PPR friendly receiver group in Drake London and Rashee Rice with real upside. The weakness is a lack of a true alpha running back, with James Cook leading a committee heavy backfield. The highlight is Harold Fannin in round eight, a tight end swing that could pay off if the target share is real. The questionable pick is rostering Sam Darnold as a third string quarterback in round fourteen when a running back lottery ticket was sitting there. Verdict: quietly capable, needs the RB room to hold.

How bout them Cowboys (John S) (C plus)

John S's strength is Amon-Ra St. Brown as a true WR1 anchor plus a deep running back rotation that could shield him from injury luck. The weakness is he never drafted a true difference making running back beyond Chase Brown, leaning heavily on committee arithmetic. The highlight is Ladd McConkey in round three, a smooth value pick behind a stacked receiver room. The questionable pick is Caleb Williams in round eight as his lone starting caliber quarterback, a shaky bet at a position he otherwise ignored. Verdict: solid bones, but this roster needs volume backs to actually show up.

I still own you (John V) (A minus)

John V's strength is an elite one two punch at the top with Ja'Marr Chase and Brock Bowers, arguably the best receiver plus tight end combo in the league. The weakness is a running back room that thins out fast after Breece Hall and Quinshon Judkins. The highlight is Brock Bowers in round two, locking down the tight end position early and never looking back. The questionable pick is drafting both Jordan Love and Aaron Rodgers as his two quarterbacks, an odd Green Bay heavy hedge. Verdict: the 2020 champion trying to remember what that felt like, and this roster gives him a real shot.

High Risk, High Reward (Matt R) (A)

Matt R's strength is a top two of Christian McCaffrey and Ashton Jeanty that might be the single best running back pairing in the league. The weakness is a receiver room that is talented at the top with Malik Nabers and Emeka Egbuka but gets speculative fast after that. The highlight is Colston Loveland in round five, a tight end bet with real upside on a crowded Chicago offense. The questionable pick is Trevor Lawrence in round ten as a lone real starter behind Jared Goff, an odd late quarterback split. Verdict: boom or bust by name and by roster.

Straight up Yorkin's it (Matt W) (B plus)

My strength is Puka Nacua and Kyren Williams giving me a Rams pipeline I am fully willing to admit could blow up in my face if that offense stumbles. The weakness is a tight end room I simply do not have, having punted the position entirely for pure running back and receiver volume. The highlight is Davante Adams still being available in round five, a veteran floor play I will happily bank on. The questionable pick, and I will own this one, is Malik Willis as a third quarterback in round thirteen when a tight end was sitting right there. Verdict: I built a roster that lives and dies with the Rams offense, which feels about right for a three time champion still chasing his fourth.

Matt's moms name (Max) (B plus)

Max's strength is Bijan Robinson leading arguably the most complete running back room outside of the very top tier, deep enough to survive a bump or two. The weakness is a lack of a true bellcow at receiver, with Nico Collins as the ceiling and a lot of complementary pieces after him. The highlight is Trey McBride in round two, locking in a top tier tight end early and removing that headache for the whole season. The questionable pick is Bhayshul Tuten in round five ahead of proven receiver depth still on the board. Verdict: quietly one of the sturdier rosters in the league.

Hawk Tua on my Penix ✝️ (Ryan) (A minus)

Ryan's strength is an absurd receiver trio of Jaxon Smith Njigba, A.J. Brown, and DeVonta Smith, three legitimate weekly starters at the top of his roster. The weakness is a running back room that is functional but unspectacular beyond David Montgomery. The highlight is Jaxon Smith Njigba at the turn in round one, a pick that already looks like a steal given the receiver run around him. The questionable pick is taking Patrick Mahomes in round ten as basically a luxury QB2 when running back depth was thinning. Verdict: the perennial best team to never win a pre Sleeper ring finally has the receiver room to match the reputation.

Will the Thrill (Tim) (A)

Tim's strength is Jahmyr Gibbs and Jeremiyah Love giving him electric, explosive running back production at the top of the draft. The weakness is a receiver room that is solid with DJ Moore and Marvin Harrison but not overwhelming at the very top. The highlight is Josh Allen falling to round three, elite quarterback production at a discount that few other teams can match. The questionable pick is Tyrone Tracy in round twelve as a change of pace back on an offense that already has committee questions. Verdict: Mr. Consistent drafted another team that looks ready to lurk into the playoffs.

Draft Power Rankings

  1. High Risk, High Reward (Matt R), the McCaffrey and Jeanty combo alone forces every other manager to game plan around him.
  2. Will the Thrill (Tim), Gibbs, Love, and a discounted Josh Allen is about as clean a draft as this league produced.
  3. JS N My Pants (Dallas), no real holes, and Travis Hunter in round thirteen is the steal of the whole draft.
  4. I still own you (John V), Chase and Bowers at the top of a roster is a legitimate championship building block.
  5. Hawk Tua on my Penix ✝️ (Ryan), three true receiver starters is a luxury almost nobody else in this league has.
  6. Sampson & Dylan-lila (Elijah), Taylor at one overall plus a stacked receiver room, the founding king is stirring.
  7. Matt's moms name (Max), Bijan and McBride locked in early, a roster with very few weak links.
  8. Straight up Yorkin's it (Matt W), Nacua and Kyren Williams give me upside, but I will admit the tight end hole is real.
  9. Real Eaters (Drew), Jefferson plus a discounted Lamar Jackson is a strong top of roster, just thin in the backfield.
  10. Serving Punt ✨ (Erica), capable and PPR friendly, but nobody in this backfield scares anyone.
  11. How bout them Cowboys (John S), St. Brown is great, but too much faith placed in unproven backs and Caleb Williams.
  12. Hampton Water (Austin), a strong running back top end undone by a receiver room that runs dry too fast.

The Back Page

If I am reading these rosters honestly, Matt R and Tim look like the two teams to beat heading into the season, with John V's receiver room and Dallas's sheer depth right behind them ready to pounce on any stumble. Ryan finally has the pieces to shed the "best team to never win" label people keep pinning on him, and Elijah looks like a man remembering what 2016 felt like. As for my own team, I built something with real juice at the top in Nacua and Kyren Williams, but I cannot in good conscience call it a top tier draft when I walked away with zero tight ends and a bench full of question marks at receiver. It is a solid draft, not a great one, and for a three time champion that admission stings more than losing does. Ask me again in November.
