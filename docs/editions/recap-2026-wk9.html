By Matthew Waters

Week Nine: The Week the GOAT Got Fleeced and Everybody Else Went Bananas

Gather round, degenerates, because Week Nine was the kind of week that makes you question whether this is a fantasy football league or an elaborate psychological experiment designed to break me specifically. I am going to cover this week with my usual ironclad objectivity, and by that I mean I am going to spend extra words on my own heartbreak because it is the biggest story here whether you like it or not. Elsewhere, Jordan Love turned into peak Aaron Rodgers for one night only, Kenneth Walker III nearly outscored an entire bench by himself, and C.J. Stroud put up a number so big it should legally be considered a crime against whoever benched him. Let's get into it.

The Recaps

F*q it we bears (Ryan) 94.16, Straight up Yorkin' it (Matt W) 90.22

Let the record show that I, your humble and completely unbiased narrator, got robbed. Puka Nacua went off for 27.0 and Javonte Williams chipped in 20.4, and it still was not enough because Kyren Williams gave me a corpse of a 7.6 and Demarcus Robinson contributed a near nothing 3.7. The real crime though sits on my own bench, where Tyler Shough was sitting on 17.7 points doing absolutely nothing for me while Matthew Stafford limped to 11.22 in the starting lineup. That is a decision I will be writing strongly worded letters to myself about. On the other side, Ryan got business class production from Christian McCaffrey (21.7) and a sneaky 16.8 from Jonah Coleman, enough to survive D'Andre Swift doing his best disappearing act at 3.1 and Kyle Pitts contributing basically a bye week stat line at 3.8. Ryan now trails our head to head series only 2 to 3, and he will sleep well tonight knowing he finally got one over on the winningest manager in league history. Enjoy it, Ryan. Savor it. It will be a while.

Matt's moms name (Max) 90.46, Serving Punt (Erica) 50.12

This one got ugly fast. Erica's lineup basically no showed, with Malik Willis turning in a quarterback line so bad (3.82) it should come with a warning label, and J.K. Dobbins adding insult at 2.7. Meanwhile her bench was sitting on a loaded gun in Harold Fannin Jr. (11.2) and Justin Herbert (10.42) that never got fired. Brutal roster management. But none of that compares to the fireworks on Max's side, where Bijan Robinson erupted for 22.7 and Denzel Boston quietly stuffed the stat sheet at 12.8, enough padding to survive the fact that C.J. Stroud was glued to his bench for a frankly absurd 35.36 points. Max won by over 40, and still left the single highest individual bench score of the entire week sitting right there. The widest margin in their series history, 40.34, just got set this very week, so somewhere Erica is replaying this one in her nightmares.

Will the Thrill (Tim) 78.66, Hampton Water (Austin) 74.84

Austin got a legitimately electric 26.6 out of Christian Watson and 18.26 from Drake Maye, but George Pickens delivered an absolute zero and Jaylen Waddle mailed in a 2.7, and that was the ballgame. Tim rode Travis Kelce's 15.3 and a sturdy 13.4 from Jahmyr Gibbs, overcoming Adonai Mitchell's goose egg in the lineup. Worth noting Tim also left Xavier Worthy (7.8) on his bench, but when you win by under four points nobody is complaining about the margins. Austin, meanwhile, continues to haunt his own history, now trailing Tim 1 to 3 in the series with a 60.84 blowout from last year still fresh in the memory banks.

It might be #over (Drew) 89.78, How bout them Cowboys (John S) 71.88

Kenneth Walker III had himself an evening, dropping 34.3 points almost single handedly into Drew's lineup, the kind of outlier week that makes waiver wire karma feel real. Caleb Douglas chipped in a tidy 10.0 as a nice bonus. John S got a genuinely strong 25.28 from Brock Purdy but was sunk by Josh Jacobs posting an actual negative half point, which is not a typo, that is a running back who cost his team production simply by existing on the field. Dak Prescott sat on John S's bench with 18.5 unused points, which stings even more given the final margin. The Cowboys continue to circle the drain at 2 and 7, now trailing Drew 1 to 4 in their head to head history.

High Risk, High Reward (Matt R) 99.38, Lamb to the Slaughter (Elijah) 70.2

Bucky Irving went nuclear for 25.5 and Rhamondre Stevenson added a cool 12.3, giving Matt R a near 100 point outing that buried Elijah despite CeeDee Lamb's monster 27.5 showing. Problem for Elijah was everybody else no showed around him: Jared Goff mustered only 5.3, Jonathan Taylor a limp 5.9, and Ashton Jeanty a quiet 4.5. That is far too much dead weight surrounding one great receiver performance. Matt R moves to 3 and 2 in that series and continues building a case that his 944.12 total points, best of anyone outside the top three, deserves a far better record than 4 and 5.

I still own you (John V) 134.48, JS N My Pants (Dallas) 96.94

This was the headline score of the week and it is not particularly close. Jordan Love went off for a preposterous 36.18, with Matthew Golden (27.4) and Ja'Marr Chase (26.5) turning John V's lineup into a highlight reel from start to finish, and Brock Bowers tacking on another 17.8 for good measure. That is four players over 17 points in a single starting lineup, which is the kind of week you frame and hang on the wall. Dallas actually played well, with Joe Burrow throwing for 29.54 and Tee Higgins adding 17.7, but you simply cannot play defense against a buzzsaw like that. The widest margin in their series, 76.24 from last year, survives for now, but 134.48 is going to be a tough number to sleep on.

Standings and Movers

JS N My Pants (Dallas) sits at the top at 6 and 3 despite the loss, climbing a spot thanks to the carnage elsewhere, with Lamb to the Slaughter (Elijah) slipping to second after his own rough week. I still own you (John V) rocketed up two spots to third off that 134.48 explosion, and Matt's moms name (Max) jumped two spots as well after torching Erica. Hampton Water (Austin) slid down one after the Tim loss, and Serving Punt (Erica) had the week's worst fall, dropping three full spots after that 50 point no show. On the other side of the ledger, High Risk, High Reward (Matt R) and F*q it we bears (Ryan) each climbed a spot off their wins. As for Straight up Yorkin' it, yes, that is me, I fell two spots to ninth, and I would like the record to show this was entirely Tyler Shough's fault for having a great game on my bench instead of in my lineup. How bout them Cowboys (John S) continues to languish, dropping to second to last at 2 and 7.

Looking Ahead

Week ten brings me a date with Will the Thrill (Tim), both of us sitting at 4 and 5 and desperate to not be the one who falls further. Elsewhere, the Battle of the Johns rivalry sits this one out, but How bout them Cowboys (John S) draws the unenviable task of hosting the division leading JS N My Pants (Dallas), and Serving Punt (Erica) tries to regroup against a red hot I still own you (John V) squad that just dropped 134. Keep an eye on Matt's moms name (Max) versus High Risk, High Reward (Matt R), a legitimate measuring stick game between two teams trending in opposite directions on the scoreboard but similar directions in the standings.

The Back Page

I want to be very clear that my loss this week was a statistical anomaly and not a reflection of my generational roster building, and I will be taking no further questions on the subject. Tyler Shough's 17.7 points sitting on my bench is simply the football gods testing my faith, the same way they tested me in 2019 and 2021 and 2023 before handing me the trophy each time. History remembers champions, not one bad Tuesday. See everyone next week, when I fully expect to be back atop this column with good news about myself, because that is simply how the universe tends to work out.
