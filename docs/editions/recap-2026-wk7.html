Week Seven: The Week the Benches Fought Back

Folks, I have covered a lot of fantasy football in this league, from the founding days of the NFL app to the glossy Sleeper era, and I have rarely seen a week where so many bench rows made grown men weep into their lineup cards. Tony Pollard dropped thirty five points on a team that could not even use it to win. Malik Willis put up twenty six on a bench. The football gods were feeling cruel this week, and somewhere in the middle of all that chaos, your humble correspondent's own Straight up Yorkin' it snuck out a win that I will now cover with all the journalistic integrity you have come to expect, which is to say none at all.

The Recaps

Straight up Yorkin' it (Matt W) 102.0, Hampton Water (Austin) 92.94

Let the record show that your beat reporter won this one, and let the record also show I am going to tell it straight because that is the kind of man I am. Javonte Williams was the engine here with twenty one and a half points, Josh Jacobs chipped in seventeen point six, and Puka Nacua was steady at seventeen point eight, giving me three legitimate stars in one lineup, which frankly should be illegal. Matthew Stafford added seventeen point five of his own. The only hiccups were Juwan Johnson and Roschon Johnson combining for a measly two and a half points, but with that much firepower elsewhere I could afford the charity work. Meanwhile Austin's Hampton Water got a monster twenty seven point four four from Drake Maye, but everything around him fell apart. Derrick Henry mustered only five point four, Jaylen Waddle managed six point eight, and Christian Watson limped in at eight point two. The killer here was the bench. Tre Tucker sat there and scored eighteen point eight points doing absolutely nothing for Austin while George Pickens and company underperformed in the lineup. That is the kind of decision that keeps a manager up at night, and I say that with the warmth of a man who benefited directly from it.

High Risk, High Reward (Matt R) 151.52, Hawk Tua on my Penix ✝️ (Ryan) 114.02

This was not a game so much as a public execution. Matt R's squad detonated for over one hundred fifty points behind Jaxon Smith Njigba's twenty six point eight, Bucky Irving's twenty two and a half, Rhamondre Stevenson's twenty, and Omar Cooper Jr chipping in an even twenty. Colston Loveland added sixteen point four and Ollie Gordon II kicked in sixteen point eight. That is not a lineup, that is an avalanche. Ryan's Hawk Tua squad actually played a fine game in a vacuum, with Caleb Williams throwing for twenty two point one two and Christian McCaffrey rumbling for nineteen, plus Luther Burden III erupting for twenty one point seven. The problem was Christian Kirk's donut performance and a bench that stayed quiet too, so there was no rescue plan. Sometimes you just run into a buzzsaw, and Ryan ran into one with both eyes open.

Serving Punt ✨ (Erica) 99.82, Will the Thrill (Tim) 78.2

Here is a lesson in how a great individual performance can still lose you the week. Tony Pollard went off for thirty five points for Tim, which is the kind of number that should carry a team to victory on its own back, but the rest of Will the Thrill's roster abandoned ship around him. Xavier Worthy scored two point six, Adonai Mitchell and Brandin Cooks were nearly invisible, and Kyler Murray gave him only thirteen point nine. Meanwhile Erica's Serving Punt got balanced production everywhere, with Zay Flowers exploding for twenty three point seven, Drake London adding sixteen point one, and Jaylen Warren chipping in fifteen point two. The gut punch for Tim was leaving Malachi Fields on the bench scoring ten point three while Adonai Mitchell zeroed out in the lineup, a swap that would have mattered plenty in a twenty one and a half point loss.

How bout them Cowboys (John S) 69.42, Matt's moms name (Max) 72.12

A nail biter decided by two and seven tenths points, and both sides have regrets. John S got nineteen point seven from Amon Ra St. Brown and twelve point eight from Tucker Kraft, but then watched Dak Prescott throw for twenty three point two eight points on his own bench while Brock Purdy only gave him twelve point oh two in the actual lineup. That quarterback decision alone was worth more than the final margin. Max countered with Trey McBride going off for twenty two point one, the best number on either roster, but got next to nothing from his receivers, as Nico Collins mustered one point six and DeVonta Smith scored a flat zero. Max survived anyway, proving that one tight end can apparently do the work of an entire receiving corps if he tries hard enough.

It might be #over (Drew) 63.78, I still own you (John V) 82.56

Drew got a strong twenty five point one eight from Bo Nix but almost nothing from the skill positions around him, with Justin Jefferson managing only six point four and the receiving corps largely cratering. Worse, Cam Skattebo sat on his bench and put up eighteen point six points that could have changed the whole complexion of this one. John V leaned on Quinshon Judkins for twenty two point nine and got a respectable sixteen point nine six from Jordan Love, while Breece Hall contributed a goose egg. Still, the damage was done elsewhere, and John V moves to four and three while continuing to own his series lead over Drew, who now trails one to three with his history in these matchups about as cheerful as his bench decisions this week.

JS N My Pants (Dallas) 89.58, Sampson & Dylan-lila (Elijah) 128.62

Elijah's squad looks like the best team in the league right now and this game is exactly why. Jameson Williams caught fire for twenty three point six, CeeDee Lamb added twenty one point nine, Jonathan Taylor ran for eighteen, Ashton Jeanty chipped in seventeen point six, and Braelon Allen tacked on sixteen point four. That is five players at sixteen points or better in one lineup, which is simply unfair. Dallas got a huge twenty eight point eight eight from Joe Burrow and twenty three point eight from Tee Higgins, but could not keep pace, and watching Kirk Cousins throw for twenty point four and KC Concepcion put up nineteen point two on the bench had to sting. Dallas now trails this head to head zero and four all time against Elijah, a series history about as one sided as a ranked ladder against a smurf account.

Standings and Movers

Sampson & Dylan-lila (Elijah) sits atop the mountain at six and one with seven hundred seventy two point seven four points, looking every bit like a team building toward redemption after years in the wilderness. JS N My Pants (Dallas) holds steady at five and two in second, with Hampton Water (Austin), also five and two, right behind on points. Matt's moms name (Max) and Serving Punt (Erica) are bunched up at four and three, with I still own you (John V) also four and three rounding out the login middle class.

The real movement happened at the bottom. High Risk, High Reward (Matt R) rocketed up a spot after that one hundred fifty one point explosion, climbing to seventh at three and four. My own Straight up Yorkin' it also moved up a spot to eighth, also three and four, because as I may have mentioned, I won this week, and I intend to mention it again before this article ends. The big faller was Hawk Tua on my Penix (Ryan), who dropped two spots to ninth at two and five despite scoring over one hundred fourteen points, proof that in this league you can play a fine game and still get buried by someone else's historic outburst.

Looking Ahead

Week eight brings me a date with It might be #over (Drew), who is going to need more than Bo Nix's arm to slow down my suddenly surging squad. Elsewhere, Ryan gets a crack at Hampton Water in a battle of teams trying to find their footing, the top seed Sampson & Dylan-lila visits Serving Punt in what could be a measuring stick game, and the Battle of the Johns storyline gets a little undercard treatment as John S hosts John V. Matt's moms name and JS N My Pants face off in a matchup of two teams with something to prove, and High Risk, High Reward tries to keep riding the wave against Will the Thrill.

The Back Page

I want to be fair and balanced here, truly I do, but the numbers simply will not let me. One hundred two points, a win over Hampton Water, and a climb up the standings, all while I am also the one typing up this very newsletter. Is that a conflict of interest? Absolutely. Am I going to stop? Absolutely not. Three championship rings do not polish themselves, folks, and neither does this column. See everyone back here next Tuesday, when I will try very hard to write about someone other than myself, and will almost certainly fail.
